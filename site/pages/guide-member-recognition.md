# Member Recognition at Private Clubs: How Staff and Members Learn Each Other's Names

*By [Julian Arceo](/julian-arceo) · FILL publish date*

Greeting members by name comes down to three things. Keep a current photo of every member in
the systems staff already use. Make learning names part of staff training and daily
briefings. And introduce members to each other with faces, not just names. This guide
covers each step, plus how to keep it current and respect member privacy.

*Disclosure: Arceo Studio Collective runs member portrait programs for private clubs, which
is one way to handle step 1. Everything else here applies whoever takes the photos.*

<!-- H2 -->
## 1. Put a current face next to every name

Your roster almost certainly has every member's name, account number, and household. What it
often lacks is a current, recognizable photo, and staff can't learn a face that isn't in
the system.

A photo is useful for recognition when it is:

- **Current.** A photo from the year a member joined may be a decade out of date.
- **Consistent.** The same background, framing, and lighting across the roster, so staff
  are comparing faces rather than snapshots.
- **Recognizable.** Light retouching only. A heavily retouched portrait flatters the member
  but doesn't help a server match a face to a name.
- **Complete.** Asking members to email a photo mostly reaches the members who would have
  sent one anyway. A scheduled sitting at the club reaches the rest.

Put the photo where staff actually look: the member lookup in your point-of-sale system,
the member roster or CRM, the staff training directory, and any check-in screens your
systems support for dining, golf, or fitness.

<!-- H2 -->
## 2. Make learning names part of the job

- **Onboarding.** New staff study the photo directory by department, starting with the
  members they'll see most often: regular diners for food and beverage, regular players for
  the golf shop.
- **Pre-shift briefings.** Show photos of the members on today's reservations, tee times,
  and event lists before service starts.
- **Hand off by name.** When a host seats a party, they pass the name along ("the Hendersons
  at table twelve") so the server can greet them by name too.
- **Check progress.** A short photo quiz at staff meetings shows who knows which members,
  and where the gaps are.

<!-- H2 -->
## 3. Help members know each other

Recognition isn't only for staff. In a club, members want to know who they are sitting
beside, and that is what makes a membership feel like a community.

- **A members-only photo directory.** Put it in the member app or portal, so members can
  look up a name they heard at dinner.
- **Introduce new members with a photo.** Use the newsletter or member portal, with the new
  member's permission. It's an easy way in at their first event.
- **Include the household.** Spouse and family portraits help staff and members put
  households together, so a member's children are greeted by name too.

<!-- H2 -->
## 4. Keep it current

- **Photograph new members as they join**, so the directory never falls behind.
- **Refresh the whole roster periodically.** Faces change, and an outdated photo is barely
  better than none.
- **Track coverage.** The percentage of active members with a current photo is the simplest
  number to watch.

<!-- H2 -->
## Privacy and consent

Member photos are personal information. Keep directory portraits in members-only and
staff-only systems. Tell members before the sitting where their photo will be used. Get a
member's permission before using their photo anywhere public, including the club's website
and social media.

## Common questions

<!-- H3 each -->

### How do private club staff learn members' names?

They start with a current photo of every member in the systems they already use, such as
the POS member lookup and the staff training directory. Then they study it during
onboarding, review the day's reservations with photos at pre-shift briefings, and pass
names along when they hand off a table or a tee time.

### What is the best way to get photos of every member?

A scheduled portrait sitting at the club, rather than asking members to send photos. A
sitting produces a consistent, current portrait, and it reaches members who would never
email one.

### Should member directory photos be retouched?

Lightly. The photo exists so staff can recognize the member. Soften blemishes and stray
details, but keep the face true to what staff will see at the door.

### How does member recognition build community at a club?

When staff greet members by name and members can put faces to names, the club stops feeling
like a facility and starts feeling like a group of people who know each other. A shared
photo directory makes that possible for staff and members alike.
