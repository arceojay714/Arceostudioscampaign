# Proposal template

Derived from the Los Altos Golf and Country Club proposal (June 2026), which is the
canonical structure. Full extracted text in `reference/los-altos-proposal-extracted.txt`.

**This supersedes `outreach/blocks/` as the primary structure.** Those blocks were written
before the real proposal was available and several of them argued for things the actual
offer does better. Keep the blocks for letter-length outreach; use this for the proposal
itself.

## Why this document works

Three things in it do the heavy lifting, and none of them are about photography:

1. **No cost to the club, no cost to members.** Stated twice, early. The club approves
   nothing, budgets nothing, and risks nothing.
2. **"No roster required."** Members book themselves through club-approved channels. This
   removes the objection that actually kills these deals — a club will not hand a vendor its
   membership list, and it should not have to. Naming that before they raise it is the
   single strongest move in the document.
3. **"Almost no lift on your side."** The three asks are explicit and small: a short
   announcement, a room, one staff contact. Concrete asks beat vague reassurance.

The tone is unhurried and declarative. No exclamation marks, no "excited to." It reads like
someone describing something that already works, which is the correct register.

## Structure — seven pages

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Cover — org name, "A PROGRAM PROPOSAL", prepared-for a **named person and their team**, prepared-by, month/year |
| 2 | **The program** — what this is · no cost to org · no cost to participants · complimentary portrait · how it's funded · almost no lift |
| 3 | **What is included** — the deliverables list · tailored to the org, they sign off on the look first |
| 4 | **Recent work** — a few portraits, labeled by type · backgrounds are yours to choose |
| 5 | **How it works** — the residency, five numbered steps · what we ask of the org (three items) · **no roster required** |
| 6 | **Member intake** — five lettered options, org picks · "what you can count on" checklist |
| 7 | **Experience** — where programs have run, grouped by geography, home region first · two named references with emails · next step · full contact block |

Page footer throughout: `ARCEO STUDIO COLLECTIVE` / `PAGE n OF 7`.

## The residency framing

Not "a portrait week." **A four to six week window**, no fixed date to commit to, built
around what works for the organization, and they set the pace. That framing lowers the
commitment enormously — the club isn't blocking a week, it's opening a window.

The five steps: the org announces → participants opt in → the session → the gift → delivered.

## The deliverables list is the competitive moat

Worth listing in full, because it's broader than a portrait program has to be and it's what
separates this from a headshot vendor:

- Finished portraits, formatted for the directory and point-of-sale
- A complimentary finished portrait for every participant, **given as the org's gift**
- Staff and board portraits, formatted for display
- **Staff training booklets** — onboarding booklets featuring team and board, so new staff
  put names to faces from day one
- Event coverage for anything falling inside the window
- Food, beverage and facility imagery
- Weekly status updates to management
- A private online gallery, one-week turnaround

The framing of the complimentary portrait **as the organization's gift to the member**
rather than as the studio's promotion is a small line doing large work. It gives the
organizer something to be generous with.

## Community segment adaptation

Same seven pages. Substitutions:

| Club version | Community version |
|---|---|
| "member" | congregant · resident · homeowner · member, per org |
| "the club" | the church · the community · the association |
| Member directory | **Pictorial directory** for churches — the term they already use |
| Staff and board portraits | Clergy and staff · leadership team · board and committee chairs |
| Club events / F&B / clubhouse imagery | Services and ministries · community events · campus and facility imagery |
| "General Manager" | Pastor · church administrator · **Life Enrichment or Activity Director** · HOA activities chair |
| Experience page: clubs list | Lead with any churches and communities served; keep the club list as credibility, since a club roster reads as *up-market* to a church |

**Photography must change.** Current examples are club members. The community version needs
the older-crowd work — the dark mottled blue backdrop, black leather posing chair, seated
three-quarter framing, individual and couple portraits. That imagery is already shot and
is a better fit for churches, senior living and 55+ than anything in the club deck.

Additions specific to the segment:

- **Churches** — say directly that the last directory is likely five or more years old, and
  that the national vendor most congregations used closed in 2021. It's true, they know it,
  and it explains why you're writing.
- **Senior living** — the consent language belongs in the proposal, not just the contract.
  Consent from the resident, or the responsible party where the resident cannot give it,
  never from staff. Also name the family-gallery path, since adult children buy remotely.
- **55+ / HOA** — these have activity committees and published boards; the "no roster
  required" point matters less because rosters are semi-public, but the "no lift on staff"
  point matters more.

## Rules for the generated proposal

- **One named recipient**, on the cover. Never "To whom it may concern."
- **Verify every factual claim about the organization** before it appears — founding year,
  anniversary, staff names, last directory year.
- The opening must be specific to that organization. If it could be sent to any org, it
  isn't ready.
- Never state a price the studio hasn't set. The Los Altos deck says options **starting
  around $150** — that is the reference point.
- References: use real, current ones. Two is enough.
