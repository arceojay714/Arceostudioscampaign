# The community segment — the second list

Churches, senior living communities, 55+ and HOA communities, lodges, and small clubs.
Tracked separately in `outreach/community-prospects.csv`.

Your instinct here is right, and the research says it's more right than you framed it.

## Why it's a different list, not more rows on the first one

Everything that makes private clubs slow is absent here.

| | Private clubs | Community segment |
|---|---|---|
| Contact info | Deliberately hidden | **Published** — staff directories, bulletins, HOA sites |
| Decision-maker | GM, sometimes a board | One person: pastor, administrator, life enrichment director |
| Gatekeeping | Heavy, by design | Light to none |
| Sales cycle | Months | Weeks |
| Order value per sitting | Higher | Lower |
| Volume per engagement | Moderate | **High** |
| Vendor competition | Two established national players | **A vacated market — see below** |

Because contact info is public, this list runs the **normal** sequence: email or call
first, no letter required. The mail-then-call sequence in `docs/contact-sequence.md` is a
workaround for club secrecy and mostly doesn't apply here.

## The finding that should change your priorities

**Lifetouch permanently closed Lifetouch Church Photography in May 2021.** Not scaled back
— closed, citing COVID impacts as insurmountable. Shutterfly, which bought Lifetouch for
$825M in 2018, kept the school and preschool business and shut the church division. Olan
Mills, the other historic giant in church pictorial directories, was absorbed by Lifetouch
years earlier and is gone as an independent.

The two national incumbents in church directory photography no longer exist.

What that leaves behind is close to ideal:

- **Tens of thousands of congregations** whose last pictorial directory is now five-plus
  years old, when the normal refresh cycle is two to three years.
- **A trained expectation of exactly your offer.** The Lifetouch church model was: free
  directory, free portrait session, free 8x10, optional purchases, no obligation. Your Tier
  3 creative already says *no sitting fee and a complimentary 5x7*. **You are not
  explaining a new business model to these buyers. You are the return of one they already
  know**, and the entire objection-handling burden disappears.
- **No national vendor to displace.** You are not fighting an incumbent relationship. You
  are filling a hole.
- **Public contacts.** Church office phone, administrator email, pastor by name, service
  times, and often the directory coordinator, all on the website.

Directory rebuilds also cluster in fall for churches, same as clubs — so this competes for
the same calendar weeks. That's a real scheduling constraint, not a footnote.

## Senior living — the best margins in the segment, handled carefully

The buyer is the **Life Enrichment Director** or **Activity Director**. That role exists to
program meaningful things for residents, is measured on resident engagement, and in many
communities is already acting as the in-house photographer for newsletters and family
communications. A professional portrait day is squarely what they are paid to find.

The economics differ from every other segment in one important way: **the resident sits,
and the adult children buy.** Often several of them, often out of state, often the first
proper portrait of a parent in a decade. That is a materially higher order value than the
sitting suggests, and it is the reason this segment is worth more than its list price.

Handle it with care, and put this in writing before the first booking:

- Consent comes from the resident where they can give it, and from the responsible party
  where they cannot. Never from the community's staff on a resident's behalf.
- Memory care requires an explicit conversation with the director about who can consent and
  who should not be photographed at all. Do not improvise this on the day.
- No resident image is used for marketing — ours or the community's — without separate
  written permission. Directory and family use is not marketing use.
- Expect to be asked about background checks and vaccination status. Many communities
  require both for vendors. Add these to `ops/club-readiness.md`.

Done properly this is the warmest, most repeatable work in the whole plan. Done sloppily it
is the fastest way to be barred from an entire regional operator.

## 55+ and HOA communities

Activity committees, published board rosters, public HOA newsletters, and a clubhouse
already set up for exactly this. Contact info is public because HOAs are legally
transparent in a way clubs are not. Same directory argument, no gatekeeper, and residents
skew toward the age where a current portrait is on the mental to-do list.

## Lodges and small clubs

Elks, Rotary, Kiwanis, Masonic lodges, VFW posts, Grange halls, historical societies.
Small budgets, published officer rosters, and almost no vendor competition. The directory
argument lands hardest here because these organizations genuinely have no photographer
relationship and often no directory worth the name.

## What this segment is *for*

It is not a downgrade from the club list. It has a job the club list cannot do.

The private club track is a **prestige engine**: long cycle, high status, and the reference
list that makes every later club say yes. The community track is a **volume engine**: short
cycle, public contacts, cash flow, and reps.

The volume engine funds and de-risks the prestige engine. Three churches and a senior
community in the fall produce revenue, a portfolio, testimonials, and a rehearsed on-site
process — all of which are exactly what's missing when a club GM asks who else you've
worked with. Right now that answer is thin, and `ops/assumptions.md` flags it as an open
question. This segment is how it stops being thin.

**Run both. Lead with this one.** It pays sooner and it makes the other one easier.
